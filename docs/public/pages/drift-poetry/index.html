Drift

A Poem in Motion

Scroll
I

The hourglass does not count —

it pours.

Each grain a small surrender,

a river learning

how to forget its name.

II

Sand holds the memory of stone —

mountains worn to whispers

by patience alone.

What takes a thousand years to fall

arrives at last

and is nothing at all.

III

Water does not grieve the shore

it leaves behind —

only pulls another tide,

only shapes the same slow turning,

only asks the earth

to keep eroding.

IV

And we — we are the water,

briefly shaped, briefly still,

pooling in the hollows

of a world that does not

need us to remain —

only to drift through

and be changed.

Nothing is lost. Everything is
merely elsewhere — waiting
to be found in a different form,
in a slower current,
in the quiet between grains.

Drift — a poem in motion
